# Ironhold

A mighty dwarven city carved deep into the [[Iron Mountains]]. Ironhold has stood for over two thousand years, its halls echoing with the ring of hammers and the songs of the deep.

The city is ruled by the [[Ironhold Council]], a body of five clan elders who meet in the [[Hall of Ancestors]]. The current head of the council is [[Thrain Ironbeard]], eldest of the [[Ironbeard Clan]].

Ironhold is the beating heart of [[Dwarven Clans|dwarven civilization]], controlling the richest mithril veins in all of [[Eryndor]].

## Districts

Ironhold is built vertically into the mountain, its districts stacked and spiraling downward from the main gate. Five major districts define the city, each shaped by centuries of expansion and the needs of different eras.

### The Gate Ward

The outermost district, just inside the [[Mithril Gate]] — Ironhold's massive main entrance. The Gate Ward is the first thing visitors see: a vast cavern with a ceiling high enough to lose in shadow, lined with statues of ancient kings. This is where the city's military garrison is quartered, and where the few non-dwarven traders and diplomats are permitted to stay. Inns, customs houses, and the offices of the [[Merchant Guild]] trade delegation occupy carved alcoves along the main thoroughfare.

### The Hearthstone

The residential heart of Ironhold. Thousands of family quarters are carved into the walls of an enormous central shaft, connected by ramps, bridges, and crank-operated lifts. Each clan maintains its own section — the [[Ironbeard Clan|Ironbeards]] hold the upper eastern face, the most prestigious address in the city. Clan halls, communal kitchens, and small shrines dot every level. The air here smells of cook-fires, pipe smoke, and the ever-present dust of stone.

### [[The Crucible]]

Ironhold's legendary forge district. A network of interconnected caverns where the heat never fades and the sound of hammers is constant. The Great Forge — a natural lava vent harnessed by dwarven engineering — sits at the district's center, providing heat and light to hundreds of smaller workshops arranged in concentric rings around it. Mithril-smithing is done only in the innermost ring by master artisans. The outer rings handle iron, steel, and stonework. Apprentices live above their workshops and rarely see daylight for years at a stretch.

### [[Deep Market]]

A vast, pillar-supported cavern where Ironhold's trade happens. Surface goods flow in through the Gate Ward — grain, timber, textiles, spices — and dwarven metalwork, gemstones, and mithril ingots flow out. The market operates on a barter-and-credit system overseen by guild assessors; coin is used mainly for dealing with outsiders. Permanent stalls are carved into the cavern walls, while the open floor hosts rotating traders. Since [[Thrain Ironbeard|Thrain]] reluctantly approved expanded trade with the [[Merchant Guild]], the Deep Market has grown noticeably, with a new wing opened to accommodate surface merchants — a development [[Bera Stonefist]] championed and traditionalists still grumble about.

### The Deep Halls

The oldest part of the city, descending toward the active mine shafts. The Deep Halls contain the most sacred spaces in Ironhold: the [[Hall of Ancestors]], where the council meets and the bones of the founding clans are interred in crystal-sealed alcoves; the Lore Vaults, where clan histories are carved into stone tablets; and the entrance to the [[Deep Roads]], the ancient tunnels connecting Ironhold to other dwarven settlements. The Deep Halls are dimly lit by phosphorescent fungi cultivated on the walls, and the air grows colder and heavier the further down you go. Only dwarves with clan business or mining duties are permitted past the Lore Vaults.

## Notable Landmarks

### [[Mithril Gate]]

The main entrance to Ironhold, a pair of doors forty feet tall faced with mithril inlay that gleams even in torchlight. The gate has never been breached — during the [[Goblin Siege of 1847]], the defenders held the Gate Ward for three months without the inner city ever being threatened. Runes of warding are carved into the stone frame, though whether they are decorative or functional is a matter of scholarly debate.

### [[Hall of Ancestors]]

The council chamber and most sacred site in the city. A natural cavern deep in the mountain, its walls lined with crystal-sealed alcoves containing the remains of Ironhold's founders and greatest heroes. Five stone thrones face a central fire pit that has burned continuously for two thousand years. The [[Ironhold Council]] meets here weekly, and any citizen may petition the council during the monthly open session. The air is thick with incense, and speaking above a murmur is considered deeply disrespectful.

### The Great Forge

The engineered lava vent at the heart of [[The Crucible]]. Dwarven engineers channeled a natural magma flow centuries ago, creating a perpetual heat source that powers the city's smithing industry. The forge is tended around the clock by the Hearthkeepers, a guild-within-a-guild who maintain the vents, manage heat distribution, and perform the rituals believed to keep the mountain's fire cooperative.

### The Lore Vaults

Below the Hall of Ancestors, rows of stone tablets record every significant event in Ironhold's history. Clan genealogies, treaties, battle records, and craft secrets are all preserved here. The Lore Vaults are maintained by the Rememberers, elderly dwarves who have memorized the tablets' contents and can recite any entry on demand.

## Daily Life

Ironhold runs on a three-shift cycle — the mountain does not know day or night, and neither does the city. Bells carved into the rock at major intersections ring to mark shift changes: the Hammer Shift (work), the Hearth Shift (rest and family), and the Stone Shift (sleep). Not everyone follows the same rotation; the city is always active, always lit by forge-glow and lantern.

**Food and drink.** Ironhold's diet relies on underground cultivation: deep mushrooms in dozens of varieties, blind cave fish from underground rivers, and thick stone bread made from a starchy root that grows in the lower caverns. Surface goods — grain, fruit, meat, ale hops — arrive through trade and are considered luxuries. Dwarven ale brewed with cave water and deep mushrooms is the everyday drink; surface-style grain ale is a recent import that younger dwarves have taken to and elders view with suspicion.

**Craft and labor.** Nearly every dwarf in Ironhold practices a craft. Mining, smithing, and stonecutting are the most respected trades, but the city also needs engineers, brewers, cooks, merchants, healers, and the Hearthkeepers who tend the Great Forge. Apprenticeships begin young and last decades. A dwarf's craft is their identity — asking "what do you make?" is the standard greeting between strangers.

**Social life.** Clan ties define social standing. The five council clans — led by the [[Ironbeard Clan|Ironbeards]] — form the upper tier, but dozens of smaller clans and clanless dwarves make up the bulk of the population of roughly fifteen thousand. Communal meals in clan halls are the primary social gathering. Music is everywhere — deep-voiced choral singing echoes through the corridors at all hours, and rhythmic hammer-songs set the pace in the forges. The annual Founding Festival, commemorating Ironhold's establishment, is the city's largest celebration: three days of feasting, craft competitions, and ritual combat in the Gate Ward.

**The tension.** The divide between tradition and modernization runs through daily life. [[Thrain Ironbeard|Thrain's]] faction sees the old ways as the source of Ironhold's strength — deep mining, clan governance, self-sufficiency. [[Bera Stonefist|Bera's]] supporters point to the growing Deep Market and argue that surface trade has made the city wealthier and more resilient. The debate plays out in council chambers, in clan halls over dinner, and in the forges where young smiths wonder whether their mithril-work should go to dwarven vaults or surface markets.
