## Published Source Book Material on Dwarven Politics

### D&D 5th Edition (2014)

**Player's Handbook (PHB)**
- Dwarven societies are divided into **clans** built along family ties and political allegiances.
- Clans are usually led by **hereditary rulers**, often monarchs descended from the clan's founder.
- In some cities a single clan dominates; in others there is a complex web of inter-clan relationships.
- Dwarves strongly value loyalty to rulers and clan above other affiliations.

**Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide (SCAG)** -- the most detailed 5e source on dwarven governance
- Covers the **Dwarfholds of the North** (Gauntlgrym, Ironmaster, Sundabar, Mirabar, etc.).
- **Mirabar** is a key example of mixed governance: a human-elected council sets trade policy, while a marchion negotiates externally. Beneath this, dwarven clans run the actual mines.
- The **Silver Marches (Luruar)** was an alliance of dwarfholds and human cities that has since collapsed due to broken obligations during the orc wars -- a useful model for inter-hold politics and fractured alliances.
- Individual dwarfholds still ally with one another but no longer under a unified pact.

**Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes (MTF)**
- Dwarves are deeply lawful, defined by family and clan.
- The clan is the foundation of everything -- individual craft serves clan glory.
- Dwarves plan multigenerationally: "we will do this for the good of the next generation."
- Elders are deeply respected; dwarves defer even to elders of other races.
- Clans tend to specialize in one or two crafts (blacksmithing, jewelry, engineering, masonry). Youth apprenticeships across clans foster unity and can last decades.

### Pathfinder (Paizo)

**Lost Omens: Highhelm**
- Introduces **12 major dwarven clans**, each focused on a specific societal role (history-keeping, smithing, magic, general labor, etc.).
- Provides detailed clan-based political structure centered on the city of Highhelm.

**Dwarves of Golarion (Pathfinder Companion)**
- Cultural and historical overviews of dwarven society.
- Lists of dwarven names, titles, equipment, and maps of enclaves and citadels.

**The Gathering Council** (Pathfinder canon)
- The ruling council of the **Five Kings Mountains** region, composed of rulers of the major dwarven cities (Larrad, Highhelm, Kovlar, Taggoret, Rolgrimmdur, Tar-Kazmukh).
- Convenes every **200 years**, though sessions have grown more contentious over the millennia.
- Presided over by the **High King of Highhelm**.
- This is a strong parallel to your Ironhold Council -- a body of leaders from distinct political entities convening for shared governance.

### Older / Third-Party Sources Worth Mining

- **"Forge and Hammer: The Secret Life of Dwarves"** (Dragon Magazine #278, by Wolfgang Baur) -- widely considered the best single article on dwarven society and culture.
- **Races of Stone** (D&D 3.5e) -- dedicated sourcebook covering dwarven and gnomish societies in depth, including governance structures.
- **The Complete Book of Dwarves** (AD&D 2e) -- has chapters on dwarven life, though the clans-and-society section is brief.
- **Heroes of High Favor: Dwarves**, **Helm & Hammer**, **Quintessential Dwarf** -- third-party supplements with additional governance detail.

## Common Governance Models Across Source Books

Surveying the published material, dwarven governance consistently uses these patterns:

| Model | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Clan Monarchy** | A single ruling clan with a hereditary king/queen | Most Forgotten Realms holds (Citadel Adbar, Citadel Felbarr) |
| **Council of Elders/Thanes** | Multiple clan leaders sharing power | Your Ironhold Council; Pathfinder's Gathering Council |
| **Guild Republic** | Craft guilds hold political power alongside or instead of clans | Mirabar's dual structure |
| **High King + Vassal Holds** | One hold has ceremonial/political primacy over others | Pathfinder's High King of Highhelm; Tolkien-influenced settings |
| **Military Meritocracy** | Leadership earned through defense of the hold | Frontier dwarfholds like Ironmaster |

## Recommendations for Fleshing Out the Ironhold Council

Based on published precedent, here are concrete elements you could develop:

1. **Council Composition**: Your five clan elders could each represent a different societal pillar (mining, smithing, warfare, trade, lore-keeping) -- mirroring Pathfinder's Highhelm clans.

2. **Succession Rules**: Is the council head always the eldest member (as implied by Thrain Ironbeard's description), or is there an election/challenge system? Published material supports both hereditary and merit-based models.

3. **The Hall of Ancestors**: Published dwarven lore consistently ties governance to ancestor veneration. The Hall could serve both as a council chamber and a sacred site where the spirits of past leaders are consulted (or believed to be present).

4. **Inter-Hold Politics**: The SCAG's Silver Marches collapse is a great template -- alliances between dwarfholds that fracture under stress. Are there other dwarven settlements in Eryndor that Ironhold has strained relations with?

5. **Guild Influence**: Even in council-based governance, craft guilds often exert pressure. The mithril trade alone would create powerful economic factions within Ironhold.

6. **Gathering Frequency**: Pathfinder's Gathering Council meets every 200 years. Your council likely meets more frequently for local governance, but consider whether there is a larger, rarer convocation of all dwarven settlements.
