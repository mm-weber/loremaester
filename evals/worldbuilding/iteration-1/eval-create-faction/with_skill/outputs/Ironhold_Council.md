# Ironhold Council

The Ironhold Council is the governing body of [[Ironhold]], the great dwarven city within the [[Iron Mountains]]. The council is composed of five clan elders, each representing one of the major dwarven clans that founded the city over two thousand years ago.

## Structure

The council operates on a principle of consensus, though the eldest member traditionally holds the tie-breaking vote and serves as the council's voice in matters of diplomacy. The current head of the council is [[Thrain Ironbeard]], eldest of the [[Ironbeard Clan]], who has led for nearly forty years.

Each elder holds their seat for life, or until they voluntarily step down. When a seat is vacated, the relevant clan selects a new representative through their own internal traditions.

## Meeting Place

The council convenes in the [[Hall of Ancestors]], a great chamber deep within [[Ironhold]] where the stone likenesses of every past council elder line the walls. Decisions made in the Hall are considered sacred and binding — to break a decree of the council is one of the gravest offenses in dwarven law.

## Current Members

- **[[Thrain Ironbeard]]** — Head of the council. A traditionalist who values craft, mining, and dwarven independence.
- **[[Bera Stonefist]]** — Thrain's chief rival on the council. Advocates for modernization and stronger ties with the surface kingdoms.
- Three additional clan elders (unnamed as yet).

## Politics

The council is currently divided between traditionalists loyal to [[Thrain Ironbeard]] and reformists led by [[Bera Stonefist]]. The core tension is whether [[Ironhold]] should remain focused inward on its mines and craft, or open itself to greater trade and alliance with surface nations. Thrain reluctantly approved increased dealings with the [[Merchant Guild]] in recent years, a concession to the reformist faction.

## History

The council guided [[Ironhold]] through the [[Dark Winter]] and the [[Goblin Siege of 1847]], both of which tested the unity of the five clans. These crises are often invoked in council debates as proof that dwarven solidarity — not outside alliances — is what keeps the city strong.
